# VISITORS — Vision and Tone

## One-sentence identity

**VISITORS is a first-person dungeon-guardian game where you are the ancient enemy inside the tomb, defending a sacred stolen artifact from generations of invaders.**

## The core reversal

The player is not the hero.

The hero is the disaster.

A visitor enters the tomb. He brings fire, steel, prayers, greed, courage, and blasphemy. He kills weak servants, breaks sacred rooms, steals offerings, and pushes toward the artifact. The player is the thing waiting inside the dark to stop him.

This reversal is the game’s spine. Everything else must serve it.

## The player

The player is the **Guardian**.

The Guardian was originally part of an Egyptian priesthood hand-selected by the Anunnaki to protect the artifact. The priesthood was chosen because it could understand fear, ritual, sacrifice, obedience, and sacred violence. Over ages, the priesthood died, transformed, or was absorbed into the tomb’s machinery.

The player remains.

The Guardian is not a generic monster. He is a bound protector, an executioner-priest, and a prisoner of his own duty.

## The tomb

The tomb is not a level selection screen.

It is the player’s cage, home, temple, body, and battlefield. The player should come to know its corridors like an animal knows the walls of its enclosure.

The tomb must feel interconnected. Shortcuts matter. Processing rooms matter. Visitor routes matter. A dead visitor’s body must be dragged through this space, not converted into abstract rewards through a menu.

## The title

**VISITORS** works as a double meaning.

1. The invaders are visitors in the Guardian’s tomb.
2. The Visitors are the ancient precursor owners tied to the artifact.

The final reveal can bend the title backward: the last invader may not be a thief. It may be an original owner returning for what was taken.

## Tone

The tone should be:

- dark fantasy
- Egyptian / Babylonian / Sumerian mythic horror
- ancient alien dread
- lonely
- brutal
- ritualistic
- hostile
- physical
- sacred but rotting

Avoid parody. Avoid cartoon villainy. Avoid cheerful dungeon-management humor. The player can be monstrous without the writing becoming silly.

## The main image

A candlelit tomb corridor. A visitor has entered with a torch. Weak servants are dead in the hall behind him. Blood marks the floor. The Guardian waits in the dark, knowing the visitor must be killed, dragged back, drained, broken, melted, and used to strengthen the tomb before the next age.

That is the game.
